Steam market analysis · 2018–2024

Steam gets 59 new games every day. Most are never seen.

I analyzed the 75,551 games released on Steam between 2018 and 2024. Each dot below is 100 games. The gold ones reached at least 500 player reviews: the games people actually found and played.

So what sets that small gold group apart? Price, genre, language, features… I asked the data one question at a time.

How many games are added each year?

The shelf got 14 times more crowded in a decade

In 2014, 1,488 games came out on Steam. In 2025, it was 21,530. As making games got easier, everyone ended up on the same storefront. That's why getting noticed gets a little harder every year.

How is player attention distributed?

Almost all the attention goes to a handful of games

Half of all games don't even reach 10 reviews. The top 1% collects 77.6% of all reviews, while the bottom half shares just 0.1%. Steam isn't a market of average games. It's a few big winners and a very quiet rest.

Do cheaper games get noticed more?

Being cheap doesn't save you

Only 5% of games under $5 get seen. In the $20–30 range, it's 29.7%. A very low price can signal low effort; a fair price says “this is a serious game.” Above $30 the rate drops again: at that price, players probably expect a familiar studio or series.

What's the strongest signal?

How many languages a game speaks shapes who plays it

Only 2.8% of single-language games get seen, compared with 23.4% of games that support 11 or more. The obvious objection: “Games with many languages are just bigger, pricier games.” To test that, I compared games within the same price range. The gap held: among $10–20 games, 7.2% of single-language titles get seen versus 44.6% of those with 7+ languages.

Which genres are less crowded?

Everyone makes Casual games; players play Action and RPGs

45.7% of releases are tagged Casual, yet they get only 17.1% of all reviews: supply far exceeds demand. Action, RPG and MMO are the opposite. Player interest there is much larger than the number of games released.

Do small details make a difference?

Signs of care come with being seen

Cloud saves, achievements, controller support, co-op… Games with these features get seen 1.5 to 4.6 times more often. To be honest, this isn't cause and effect: teams that care about their work tend to do all of these at once. Still, they're the first signs on a store page that tell players “this team takes its work seriously.”

Where does Turkish stand?

Turkish support is growing slowly but steadily

In 2014, 4.2% of new games supported Turkish; in 2025, 13.8%. Among games that get seen, it's nearly one in four. Successful studios already count on Turkish players. For smaller teams, there's still open ground.

So what should you do?

What I'd tell an indie developer

  • Build the game to be translatable from day one. Language support is the strongest signal, regardless of price. Keeping text out of the code makes adding 7 languages later cheap. Put Turkish on that list too.
  • Don't try to stand out by lowering the price. Games at $10–30 get seen 4–6 times more often than those under $5.
  • In a crowded genre, craft is the difference. If you go Casual, quality and a strong hook will decide it. RPG and Action, where demand outweighs supply, are a less crowded door.
  • Don't skip the “small” things. Cloud saves, achievements and controller support take a few days, but they show care on the store page.
  • Don't overthink the launch month. The gap between the best and worst month is just 3.3 points. Language support can move the rate from 3% to 23%.

Method and limits

Data

The public Steam Games Dataset (MIT license), collected from the Steam API and SteamSpy: 114,322 games. Software and tools were removed. The data runs until September 8, 2026.

What does “seen” mean?

A game with at least 500 user reviews. By a widely used industry estimate, each review corresponds to roughly 30–60 sales, so this threshold is about 15,000–30,000 sales.

Why 2018–2024?

Recently released games haven't had time to collect reviews yet. Including them would make success rates look lower than they are. Yearly release counts also include 2025.

Limits

These relationships are correlations, not cause and effect. Review count is a rough proxy for sales. Only Steam is covered; consoles and mobile are not.
